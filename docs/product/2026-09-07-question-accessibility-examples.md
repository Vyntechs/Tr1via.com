# Question accessibility: a concrete first category

**Status:** editorial examples written for review on September 7, 2026. These are authored examples of the intended style, not output from a live run of the modified generator. Answers and reveal facts were checked against the linked NASA sources. Difficulty labels are editorial estimates, not measured audience results.

The category is **Our solar neighborhood**. The first three questions give players familiar ways in, the middle three introduce more challenge, and the last rewards deeper knowledge. The seven-question board and its point values remain recognizable.

## 100 points — approachable, estimated difficulty 1

**Which planet is the largest in our solar system by diameter?**

A. Saturn · B. Jupiter · C. Uranus · D. Neptune

**Answer: B. Jupiter.**

**Reveal:** Jupiter is about eleven times as wide as Earth.

**Why it belongs:** a familiar fact provides a legitimate early success. All four choices are giant planets. A player can use remembered size comparisons without knowing exact measurements. [NASA: Jupiter facts](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-facts/).

## 200 points — approachable, estimated difficulty 1

**Which planet is nicknamed the Red Planet?**

A. Venus · B. Mercury · C. Mars · D. Neptune

**Answer: C. Mars.**

**Reveal:** Its reddish appearance comes from iron minerals that have oxidized—essentially, rust.

**Why it belongs:** recognition is welcome. The extra detail comes in the reveal, so an accessible question still teaches something. [NASA: Mars facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/).

## 300 points — approachable, estimated difficulty 2

**At full moon, the bright face we see is reflecting light directly from which object?**

A. Earth · B. Mars · C. Venus · D. The Sun

**Answer: D. The Sun.**

**Reveal:** Moonlight is reflected sunlight; the Moon does not produce its own visible glow.

**Why it belongs:** familiar everyday observation supports a short reasoning step. The full-moon wording avoids confusing this with earthshine on a crescent Moon. [NASA: Moon viewing tips](https://science.nasa.gov/moon/viewing-tips/).

## 400 points — moderate, estimated difficulty 3

**What is the main reason Earth has summer and winter?**

A. Its changing distance from the Sun · B. Its tilted axis as it orbits the Sun · C. Changes in the Sun's heat output · D. Changes in Earth's rotation speed

**Answer: B. Its tilted axis as it orbits the Sun.**

**Reveal:** Earth's tilt changes the angle and duration of sunlight each hemisphere receives during the year.

**Why it belongs:** the distractors express understandable misconceptions. Knowing that the hemispheres have opposite seasons gives a player a useful reasoning path. [NASA: What causes the seasons?](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/en/).

## 500 points — moderate, estimated difficulty 4

**Saturn's bright main rings are made mostly of which material?**

A. Iron fragments · B. Silicate rock · C. Water ice · D. Carbon dust

**Answer: C. Water ice.**

**Reveal:** Those apparently smooth bands contain enormous numbers of separate chunks of ice.

**Why it belongs:** this asks one interesting property of a familiar sight, without requiring a mission date, named gap, or exact measurement. [NASA: Cassini and Saturn's rings](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/cassini/science/rings/).

## 600 points — moderate, estimated difficulty 5

**Which rocky planet has the hottest surface, thanks to its thick, heat-trapping atmosphere?**

A. Venus · B. Mercury · C. Earth · D. Mars

**Answer: A. Venus.**

**Reveal:** Venus's powerful greenhouse effect makes its surface hotter than Mercury's, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.

**Why it belongs:** the atmosphere clue lets someone use partial knowledge. The question rewards understanding beyond simply recalling which planet is closest to the Sun. [NASA: Venus facts](https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/).

## 700 points — stretch, estimated difficulty 6

**Which planet rotates almost on its side, with an axial tilt of about 98 degrees?**

A. Neptune · B. Saturn · C. Jupiter · D. Uranus

**Answer: D. Uranus.**

**Reveal:** Uranus's extreme tilt makes its rotation look more like rolling than the upright spinning we usually picture.

**Why it belongs:** this rewards deeper knowledge with a memorable fact. It occupies the deliberate stretch slot. [NASA: Uranus facts](https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/facts/).

## How the editorial decision changes

The following old-style stems are illustrative examples of specialist recall, not claims about questions served in a particular live game.

| Recall-heavy treatment | Proposed treatment | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ask for Jupiter's diameter as an exact number | Ask players to identify the largest planet and explain its scale after reveal | Familiar knowledge earns an early success; precision is unnecessary |
| Ask for Earth's axial tilt to a decimal place | Ask why Earth has seasons | A player can connect facts and reason |
| Ask for Venus's average surface temperature | Describe its heat-trapping atmosphere and ask which planet it is | Accurate context supplies a useful clue |

This is a first category, not a declaration that space questions are the right topic for every room. The same structure should apply to films, food, music, sports, and local interests. Content should still be varied and surprising; the entry points make that variety enjoyable to attempt.
